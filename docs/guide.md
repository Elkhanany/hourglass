# Using Hourglass

How to use Hourglass, in full. The [README](../README.md) has the short
version: what it is, how to install it, and the controls.

## Zen

Zen is yours to enter and to leave. Spread two fingers anywhere on the
screen, or tap the moon icon, and the controls fade over about a second and a
half to 12 % (or disappear entirely with *Full Zen*) as the glass grows to
fill the screen; while they are dimmed the tenths rest. Pinch two fingers
together and they come back at once (`Esc` on a keyboard; a trackpad's pinch
works both ways too). Nothing else changes the view. A tap on the glass
starts or pauses it in Zen as it does at home; playing with the glass
(moving, turning, spinning, catching, mending, knocking) leaves the view as
it is; the controls do not fade by themselves however long the sand runs;
and the end of a glass leaves Zen as it is. The fingers must travel a little
(a fifth of the gap between them, and 36 px at least), a glass that is
already being carried or turned is not let go for a second finger, and
turning back within the same pinch undoes it. The first three times, the
moon icon says how to come back. Where the browser allows it, Zen also goes
full screen. With *Reduce Motion* turned on in the system settings, all of this
happens at once instead. With *Reduce Motion* a glass that is let go glides
home instead of falling, nothing flashes and the phone's shaking cannot crack
the glass; what a finger does to it (a slam on the desk, knocks, hard spins)
still counts.

When the sand runs out while the controls are dimmed, the glass stays still
through the last grains and the bowl, then the controls return slowly. A
touch brings them back at once. The app itself opens by fading up from dark,
with the glass already in its place.

## The amount of sand

The amount of sand shows the time. An hour is the full glass, and a shorter
glass holds less by the square root of its time: four times the time is twice
the sand. A minute is a tenth of a bulb, five a good fifth, ten a third,
fifteen two fifths, thirty over half. (Strictly in proportion, a minute would
be a pinch and every glass would run with the thin thread of the hour glass.)
Each shape of glass (Tune, Glass) has its own full glass, as much as fits
under its neck. Delta, whose low ends hold less, is full at an hour with no
room to spare: there the **Sand** button reads **Full**, and a tap on it says
that the glass is full. Any glass reads so once sand has been poured in to
the brim.

## Tune

**Tune** holds the rest, in four tabs: **Sand**, **Glass**, **Sound** and
**Time**. The sheet covers the lower half of the screen and stops just under
the neck of the glass, on every tab, so the upper bulb, the neck and the
stream stay in view and a new sand, finish, layered sand or breathing glow
is seen as it is chosen. A tab too long for the sheet scrolls; the tab row
stays where it is. While a tab has more below, its rows fade into the
bottom of the sheet and a small arrow there points down; it goes once you
reach the end. Tune opens on the tab used last, at its top. Tap outside
the sheet, the close button or `Esc` to close it; on a keyboard the arrow
keys move between the tabs.

**Sand**

- **Colour**: the sand itself, chosen apart from the frame (from release
  1.14.0). Nine sands: Sahara Quartz, Volcanic Black, Moonstone, Lapis Lazuli,
  Rose Quartz, Sea Glass, Red Garnet, Olivine and Amethyst, and **Custom**, a
  sand made from any colour. Tap Custom and the sand turns sea green, and a
  row **Your colour** appears under the swatches: tap its circle to open the
  phone's colour picker, and the sand follows the colour as you move it. The
  circle shows the colour the sand really uses: a very pale or very dark
  colour is softened so the sand still shows against the dark room. The
  colour is kept when you choose another sand, and Custom brings it back.
  The name at the top of the screen steps through the nine sands (and your
  own, once you have made one); the frame keeps its finish.
  A glass set up before 1.14.0 keeps its look: its material's sand, and its
  frame's finish (Volcanic Black keeps its brass caps, Lapis Lazuli its gold,
  and so on).
- **Layered sand** gives the sand a second tone. A new glass is filled in a
  pattern drawn for it alone (bands fine or bold, level or leaning, arched,
  waved or folded), what falls settles into strata on the heap, and sand
  added with **Sand** arrives in layers of the other tone. It is off by
  default, and with it off the sand is exactly as it was. Turning the glass
  over tumbles the sand, and within about four seconds it settles into a new
  pattern, drawn afresh for each bulb at every turn.
- **Real sand** (on by default) runs the sand on the phone's graphics chip
  (see "Real sand" below; "[Real sand: the switch in
  Tune](engines.md#real-sand-the-switch-in-tune)" says how it works);
  turning it on or off builds the glass afresh, keeping the running time,
  and its note says how it is doing.
- **Grain** is Classic, Fine (×2) or Finest (×4): how many grains the same
  glass holds. It sets the classic sand, so it shows only while the classic
  sand is the one that runs (Real sand off, or not available this time).
  Another grain is another lattice, so choosing one builds the glass afresh
  behind a short fade; the running time is kept, a crack is not. Finer sand
  is more work for the phone: if the glass ever stutters on Finest, Fine has
  half as many grains for about a third of the work.
- **In the hand: Real motion**: the sand feels what the phone feels. Lean it
  and the piles slump, lay it on its side and the flow (and the clock) stop,
  turn it over and the other bulb becomes the top. Walk and the falling
  thread sways; shake it and the sand lifts and lands; twist it and the sand
  sloshes; spin it flat on a table and the sand is pushed to both ends.
  With the classic sand, shaken hard for about three seconds, in any
  direction, the glass creaks and then cracks (its note says so only then).
  An iPhone forgets its permission for this whenever the app is closed, so
  after a fresh launch a small tilt icon waits in the top bar: tap it and
  allow, or leave it and the glass stands still that time.
- At the foot (the last line of the Sand tab), how many grains there are,
  and the version: "Version 1.17.0" when the copy installed for offline use is that same release. "Offline
  copy not installed yet" after it means the app does not yet work offline
  (open it once more with a connection); "update ... ready: close and
  reopen" means a newer release has arrived, and the next launch runs it;
  "offline copy" with another number means the installed copy is another
  release than the page.

**Glass**

- **Shape**: the glass itself. Classic is the teardrop the app has always
  had; Lozenge, Delta, Ampoule, Trumpet, Florence, Onion, Bell and Gourd are
  the others. The shape in use is drawn large on the left, and beside it
  every shape is drawn small, all at the same scale, so a wide glass looks
  wide and a small one small. A tap on a shape only shows it there: nothing changes yet, so
  you can look at them one after another. **Use this shape** builds the glass
  in that shape: the glass fades, comes back in its new shape, and Tune opens
  again on this tab. A running glass keeps its time: the sand is counted
  anew for the new glass, as the same share of what it holds when full, and
  it runs out on time. Trumpet and Florence have a long narrow tube above
  the neck: the last of the sand stands in it as a column that sinks as it
  runs, and the glass runs out on time like the others.
- **Show the frame**: the turned posts and caps around the glass. Off, only
  the glass remains, a little larger.
- **Finish** (under it, while the frame is shown): what the caps and posts
  are made of, whatever the sand: Walnut (the default), Brass, Silver, Gold,
  Copper or Driftwood.

**Sound**

- **Sound**: the volume (the speaker at the top of the screen mutes it).
- **Ambience**: None, Brook, Rain, Ocean, Waterfall or Drips behind the
  sand (the one you tap plays long enough to be heard), and its own level
  under the cards while one is chosen.
- **Fade out before the end** (while an ambience is chosen): the ambience
  falls away over the glass's last minute, last 5 minutes or last 10 minutes
  (turned on, it starts at 5), so the glass ends in quiet; the sand and the
  bowl are not touched. It follows the time really left: paused, it holds;
  sand added or the glass turned brings it back up, softly. A glass shorter
  than twice the stretch fades over its last half instead (a 4-minute glass
  set to 5 minutes fades over its last 2), so it is heard whole for at least
  its first half. In work and rest only the very end fades: between the
  phases the ambience goes on as it is.
- **Sand sound**: six voicings of the falling sand.
- **Sand**: how loud the sand's own sounds are (the stream, its grains, the
  glass ringing, thumps, slides and the slosh, with either sand), from 0 to
  100. 100 is the sand as it has always been; the bowl and the ambience keep
  their own levels.
- **End of the glass: Completion bowl**, a soft singing-bowl bloom when the
  last grain falls. While it is on:
  - **Rings**: *Once* (as it always was), *Three times* (again about 8.5 s
    and 17 s later) or *Until a tap* (again every 8.5 s, for 10 minutes at
    most). While the bowl is still to ring again, the first tap anywhere, or
    the first key, only stops it: it does nothing else (it does not start a
    new glass or press the button it lands on). The next one works as always.
    The rings keep the screen on only with *Keep screen awake* on. Nothing
    can ring while the phone is locked or the app is in the background; come
    back and a ring whose time has passed is not played late, the next comes
    on its time. Between the phases of work and rest the bowl rings once, as
    before: *Rings* is for the last end. Reset, another duration, a turn or a
    new start stop it too.
  - **Bowl**: the bowl's level, 0 to 100. 50 is the bowl as it always was,
    100 doubles it (6 dB asked; about 5 dB heard, since the app's limiter
    holds the peak back so nothing clips), 0 is silent.
- **Sound in silent mode**: on (as it always was), the app sounds with the
  ringer switched off, as a timer should. Off, it follows the ringer switch,
  and music playing in another app plays on under it. Where the browser does
  not let a page choose, the row says so: the phone decides by itself.

**Time**

- **Quick durations**: the six pills under the glass, shown here as six
  buttons in the same order (1, 5, 10, 15, 30 and 60 minutes as the app
  comes). Touch one and the custom-duration dial opens with its minutes only;
  turn it to any whole number of minutes from 1 to 99 and tap **Set**, and
  the pill under the glass changes at once. Close the dial instead (its close
  button, `Esc` or a tap above it) and the pill stays as it was. The six stay
  in the order you set them, and two may hold the same number. A pill is lit
  when its minutes are the glass's (the first of two the same); changing a
  pill never changes the glass, set or running: it keeps its time, and if no
  pill has its minutes any more, Custom is lit, as it is for any time of your
  own.
- **The clock: Show when it ends** (off as the app comes): while a glass
  runs, the line under the time says when it will reach `00:00.0`, as
  *Flowing · ends 14:35*, in the phone's own 12 or 24 hour style. It is the
  end of the glass that is running now: sand added, a turn, a pause or a new
  duration moves it, and it is gone while the glass is paused, turning or
  finished. In a work and rest sequence it is the end of the phase that is
  running (*Work · 1 of 4 · ends 14:35*), not of the whole sequence. Breathing
  pace changes no time, so it changes no end. With *Real motion*, a glass
  held on its side stands still, so its end moves later for as long as it
  lies there. Zen dims it with the rest of the line, and on a short screen,
  where that line is not shown, it is not shown either. A screen reader
  hears the state as before; the end is in the time's own label ("Time
  remaining, ends 14:35"), and is never read out by itself.
- **Keep screen awake** while the sand is flowing, and for 12 s after it
  runs out, or for as long as the bowl is still to ring again (Screen Wake
  Lock API).
- **Breathing pace**: while the sand is running, the warm light behind the
  glass swells and brightens through the in-breath, holds, and fades through
  the out-breath, slowly and without a sound. Choose *Calm* (in 4 s, out 6 s:
  six breaths a minute), *Box* (in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4) or *Deep* (in 4,
  hold 7, out 8). A breath begins with the in-breath when the glass starts,
  and the glow eases back to rest when the glass is paused, finished or
  reset. It works in Zen and Full Zen; with *Reduce Motion* the glow only
  brightens and dims.
- **Full Zen**: Zen hides the controls completely instead of dimming them.
- **Touch: Haptics**: a light tick from taps on the glass, buttons and
  switches, where the phone supports it. On a phone that can vibrate it also
  covers the end of a glass, landings and cracks. Turn it off and all of them
  stop (see "Browser notes" below for an iPhone).
- **Put the look and sound back as they came**: tap it, then tap again
  within three seconds ("Tap again to put them back"). The sand (Sahara
  Quartz; a colour of your own is kept for its swatch), the frame and its
  finish (walnut), layered sand, the volume, the sand sound and the Sand level
  (100), the ambience, its level and its fade before the end (off), and the
  completion bowl, its Rings (Once) and the Bowl level (50) go back to how the
  app came; everything else stays as you set it (Real sand and the grain, the
  glass's shape, the durations and the six quick durations, *Show when it
  ends*, work and rest, breathing, Zen, the screen, haptics, Real motion,
  Sound in silent mode, the mute button), and the running glass carries on.
  Sound in silent mode is kept because it is how the phone treats the app:
  put back on, it could make the phone ring in a quiet room.

The glass also remembers where it was: close or reload the page and a running
timer carries on from the wall clock, a paused one comes back as it was left,
cracks and gold seams included.

An update arrives whole or not at all: the installed app keeps running the
release it has until the new one is complete, so it always opens offline.
Should it ever open offline and say *Hourglass is updating. Open it again
with a connection.*, a file of its offline copy went missing; the next
launch with a connection fills the copy again. With a connection it says
*Hourglass is updating. Open it again in a moment.*: the new release is
on its way in. Close the app and open it again a little later.

## Work and rest

The Custom duration sheet has two more rows under the dial. **Then rest** adds
a rest of 1 to 60 minutes after the dial's time (Off by default; hold + or −
to run through the numbers). Once a rest is set, **Rounds** says how often the
pair is repeated, 1 to 12. The Set button and the Custom pill say what is set:
`Set 25:00, rest 5, ×4` and `25·5 ×4`.

A sequence is rounds × (work, then rest). The readout counts down the phase
that is running, and the line under it names it: *Work · 2 of 4*, *Rest · 2 of
4* (just *Work* and *Rest* for a single round). When a phase that is not the
last runs out, a soft bowl sounds as its last grain lands (the completion
bowl, lighter and shorter, and only with *Completion bowl* on), the glass
turns itself over with the Flip button's unhurried half turn, and the next
phase starts as the turn ends. It is the same sand, timed to the new length:
through a 5 minute rest it runs five times as fast as through the 25 minutes
before it. The last phase ends as any glass does. The turn leaves the view
as it is, in Zen or out of it. With *Reduce Motion* the glass does
not turn: the sand fades away below and returns above, in the same time.

Pause and resume work inside a phase; paused between two phases, the turn
waits. **Flip**, or a turn by hand, swaps the time within the phase as on any
glass and skips nothing; **Sand** adds time to the phase. **Reset** goes back
to the start of the sequence, not started. Flipping a finished sequence starts
it again from its first glass. A preset, or a custom time with rest Off, ends
the sequence.

The wall clock stays the master here too. If the page was hidden or the phone
asleep through one or more phase ends, no turn is replayed: on return the
glass is in the right phase with the right time left and its sand in place,
and at most one soft bowl sounds, only if the last phase end was a few seconds
ago. A reload restores a running or paused sequence the same way. On an iPhone
a locked screen stops the page, so the bowl between phases is heard only while
the app is on screen (*Keep screen awake* is on by default while the sand is
flowing).

## Playing with the glass

The glass is an object you can hold. Take it by its waist and it goes where
the finger goes, lifting off its shadow, as far as it still fits on the
screen. It stands on a desk: it cannot be pushed below its resting place or
past the edges of the screen, and pushed against one it stops there. Let go
in the air and it falls, at the sand's own slow-motion gravity, lands with a
small bounce (two at most) and is centred again; let go while moving and it
flies on, and can strike an edge. While it falls its sand falls with it, and
the stream breaks; when it lands the sand jumps and lands too.

Take it by a bulb and it turns; flick it and it spins on by itself, slowing
as its sand is flung outward, and settles upright or inverted. Each half turn
swaps the time, as the Flip button does. Touch it while it moves and it stops
under your finger, and stays where it stopped until the finger moves it on;
but a finger that lands on a bulb of a spinning glass and moves on with the
spin within 80 ms carries it along, so flick on flick builds speed. The sand
feels all of this at the true scale of the glass on the screen, so a gentle
drag barely stirs it and a hard spin pins it to both ends. Its own motion it
keeps: carry the glass off quickly and the sand is left behind for a moment,
the falling thread with it; stop it dead, or let it strike an edge, and the
sand surges on, piles against the glass and runs back to its slope.

What you hear. Sand that moves is heard, whether the glass is running or
not: before it is started, while it is paused and after it has run out, as
much as while it runs. Shake it and the bed lifts and lands with a soft
thump (a hard landing also buzzes, on Android), sand thrown against the far
end of the glass clacks, and the stirred grains shimmer. Lean it past the
sand's slope, or carry it about, and the sand slides with a soft hiss on the
side it runs to: leaned to the right, in the right ear. In a glass that only
stands there (not running) that hiss is heard from a small slide on, well
under the stream's own level, and when such a glass is turned over (the Flip
button before the start, or while paused) the slide is heard through the
turn and on until the sand rests; under a running stream small slides stay
beneath the stream, as they always did, and only a turned or shaken bed
rushes. Nothing sounds while no sand moves.
The stream and the ambience still begin only when the glass runs, and the
mute button and the volume apply to all of it. On an iPhone the sound of
each launch begins with the first touch that ends: any touch will do (a
tap, the tilt icon, the glass taken and let go), and one is enough.

## Breaking it

All of this is the classic sand's glass. While Real sand runs (the default
where the phone has WebGPU) the glass does not crack: see *What it leaves out
for now* under [Real sand](#real-sand).

Everything that strikes the glass strikes it at some speed, and the speed
decides what happens (on an iPhone; px are CSS pixels on screen):

| What you do | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Set it down, or let it go from under about 60 px | The sand jolts, without a sound. Never a mark. |
| Let it go from about 65 to 110 px (it lands at 700 to 920 px/s) | It creaks, keeps about a third of a crack's worth of damage and shows a stress mark. About five such drops in a row crack it. |
| Let it go from the top of the screen, or bang it on the desk or an edge at 920 px/s or more | It cracks at once: 16 to 24 cells long, longer the harder the blow. |
| Bang it down at 1,800 px/s or more, or strike a cracked place again | It breaks through, and sand leaks out and gathers on the desk. Each further blow widens the breach. |
| Eight quick knocks (taps under 0.4 s apart) near one place | Creaks from the fifth, a crack on the eighth, in one and a half to two and a half seconds. |
| Three or four hard flicks in a row | A crack on a dome. Casual flicks do nothing. |
| Stir it round at two turns a second | Creaks after about a second, a crack in about three. |
| Shake the phone hard (3 g, 3 to 5 times a second) with Real motion on | Creaks at about 2 s, a crack at about 3 s, a chip soon after, whichever way it is shaken. |

It is hard to break by accident. Single, double and triple taps, taps up to
two a second, gentle dragging, casual flicks, the Flip button, walking,
running, setting the phone down, tapping it on a palm and a firm shake leave
no damage at all.

Damage is seen, heard and felt before the glass cracks. A pale stress mark
shows where the glass is loaded, from a fifth of the way to a crack; it
grows from about 5 to 20 px and fades as the glass heals (in a few seconds
to a quarter of a minute). The glass creaks, louder and higher each time, at
35, 60, 80 and 92 % of the way; on an iPhone each creak made by a finger
also ticks in the hand. The first time a finger makes it creak, a quiet note
says that the glass can crack. A new crack flashes for a little over half a
second and is 16 cells long or more, on the face of the glass, and for the
first three cracks a note says how to mend it.

Hold a finger still on a cracked glass and gold flows into the crack and
seals it (kintsugi), without stopping the timer; a mended place takes no
more, but the glass can crack again beside it, three times in all. Reset, or
a new duration, gives a fresh glass.

A burst of knocks leaves the timer alone: the first tap of a burst starts or
pauses the glass like any tap, and the second takes that back. Knocks also
reach a glass whose controls are dimmed (the first one brings the controls
back, as any tap does).

## Real sand

**Real sand**, in **Tune**'s Sand tab under the sands, runs the sand on the
phone's graphics chip: the sand of the sand lab, every grain a point of a
simulated continuum, in the same glass. It is on by default from version
1.10.0, and the classic sand (the sand as it has always been) is the
fallback. An install from before then finds it on once, whatever it had,
since those versions saved it off at every launch; turned off after that,
it stays off. Turning it on or off loads the page again, as another grain
does, and the running time is kept. The app never turns it off by itself.

What stays as it was: the clock (the last grain leaves the neck at
`00:00.0`; in the sand lab on an iPhone it left within 5 ms of it in every
run), the glass and its frame, the six sands and layered sand, Flip,
turning and spinning the glass, **Sand**, **Reset** with its one-second
transition, work and rest, Real motion with the phone lying still or in the
hand, the glass carrying on across reloads, the sound of the stream, the
landings, the tail and the fill, the bowl, and every haptic.

What you hear of the sand itself, from version 1.11.0 (until then Real
sand's bed was silent). What "Playing with the glass" says of the sound
holds for Real sand too, and it is made from what the sand on the graphics
chip did, a few frames after it did it: a bed that falls and lands thumps,
one thrown against the far end clacks, sand sliding within its bed hisses
on the side it runs to, and stirred sand shimmers, about as loud as the
classic sand for the same motion, before the start, paused and finished as
while it runs. A bed is the sand that lies together: the thin stream, the
first grains on the floor of a glass just started and the last above its
neck make no sound of a bed, so a glass that is started and left alone
sounds of its stream only; and a bed that is thrown flies without a hiss
and lands with its thump. A phone that moves without moving its sand makes no sound of
sand: walked with, the sand on the chip lies still, and is silent, where
the classic sand shimmers a little. And leaned, its heaps stand steeper
than the classic sand's and hardly slide (one of the lab's known faults,
below), so a lean alone is all but silent with Real sand for now, where the
classic sand's avalanche hisses; a shake, a jolt, a flick or a turn is
heard. A glass asleep (its sand at rest) is silent, and the sound never
keeps it awake; a jolt wakes it and is heard as the sand moves.

What it needs: WebGPU, which Safari has from iOS 26, also in a web app on
the home screen. Without it the switch shows off and says *Needs WebGPU (iOS
26 or later). Tap to try again*, and the classic sand runs; where the only
graphics adapter is a software one, it says the device's graphics are too
slow. The switch is never greyed: a tap on it tries again. A browser with
WebGPU but no usable adapter is remembered by its user agent for seven
days, so later launches say so without loading the engine; after that, or
once an iOS update has changed the user agent, a launch tries again (and one
that finds no adapter right after the graphics chip was reset or stopped
answering is not remembered at all). So does an app update
that brings a new engine, which also forgets three failed starts. A start
that fails this way while the app is put away is not believed: it is tried
again once the app is in view.

Starting. The dark opening waits up to 1 s longer for Real sand's first
picture, so that most launches open straight on Real sand. If the graphics
chip takes longer, the opening fades up as before on the classic sand,
which shows and runs while the chip gets ready, and the two cross-fade once
Real sand has drawn its first picture: on an iPhone that took
0.6 s in Chrome and 3.4 s in a home-screen web app opened cold. A slow start
is never counted as a failure.

A graphics chip that stops answering. If, while the glass runs on screen,
Real sand sends nothing back for 4 seconds (its picture would freeze
while the clock runs on), the classic sand takes over at once, at the same
time and with the same sand above, with a note; Real sand starts again
2 s later. The third time within ten minutes, the classic sand stays until
the app is opened again. A glass that is idle, paused or finished and at
rest sends nothing back and is never taken for one that stopped.

Moving the glass. The sand answers the phone at once: a flick, a shake,
the hand's jitter. Real sand lives in a slowed world (a fall takes about
3.5 times longer than real), and until build 5 it played your motion back
as a slowed film of it, which under any shaking or handling ran half a
second behind the phone, and softly. Now the part of a push along the
sand's floor that would slide a resting bed (beyond its friction, about
0.6 g) is played at once, as fast as the film made it, and anything
gentler is felt just as it is, as is every push up or down against the
floor (so a shake up and down heaps the bed, as the film did, and does not
throw it up in a column); the floor is where the sand's gravity points,
however you hold the phone (upright, on its side, upside down). The
sharpest peaks are held to 2 g and paid out over the next few hundredths
of a second, so a jerk moves the bed without tearing it. Measured in the
sand lab: a flick moves the bed 42 ms after the hand, where it took 108,
and the sand stops sloshing about 0.1 s after you stop shaking, where it
went on for half a second. What this trades: a lone jerk of a glass at
rest moves its bed at once and as fast as the film did, but about a third
as far, and the stop ends the slide; in the film (and in the sand's own
physics at real speed) the bed is thrown against the far wall and the stop
throws it on to the other one. A glass asleep (paused or finished, the
sand at rest) wakes in the frame a jolt comes, where a flick never woke
it. With Reduce Motion on, the glass's motion is felt as it is. The engine
also keeps the slow-motion playback for a Tune choice later, "Moving the
glass: Live | Slow motion", Slow motion reading: "A bump to a glass held
still replays slowly, like a high-speed film; once you shake or handle it,
the sand moves at once." (The sand lab's "jolts" button tries them both
now.)

Battery and heat. Nothing runs on the graphics chip while nothing moves (a
glass paused or finished and at rest, or the page hidden). While the glass
is turned, handled or shaken, as a glass starts and in its last seconds,
and for 2 s after any of these, it runs at the full rate: 480 steps of the
sand a second, more (up to 1,440) while a bed of sand moves fast, drawn
every frame. A glass running calmly, the phone lying still or held still in
the hand, is drawn 30 times a second at the same 480 steps. A glass that
the chip has kept below 0.6 of real time for 20 s drops to that calm rate
(the heat guard). Calm saves little on a long glass, since its steps are
most of the work: the 60-minute glass kept the iPhone's graphics chip 85 %
busy in the sand lab, and 79 % calm. So when the phone is hot the sand may
simply run slower than real time for a while; the clock stays exact, and
the report (below) logs each time the guard trips. There is no economy
mode: it uses more battery than the classic sand, most on long glasses.

Keep the screen awake. While the phone is locked or another app is in front,
Real sand stands still and the clock does not; back on screen, the sand
the clock says has run meanwhile is moved below at once. **Keep screen
awake** (on by default) holds the screen on while the sand flows, with Real
sand as without it. (The sand lab has neither, and its 4-minute glass ended
7 minutes late when the phone locked itself.)

What it leaves out for now:

- The glass does not crack. A hard landing or a blow thumps and clacks;
  knocks, flicks and shaking do nothing to it. Cracks the glass already had
  stay, leak nothing and can be mended; Reset makes it whole.
- The classic sand's glow, rim light and depth shading are not drawn; the
  grains are lit as in the sand lab.
- **Grain** sets the classic sand only, so it is hidden while Real sand
  runs; Real sand keeps its own grain.
- The sand lab's known faults: a turned bed slides round the glass as a
  block before it breaks up, a bed at rest creeps, heaps stand steep.

The note under the switch says how it is doing:

| Note | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| *Starting…* | the graphics chip is getting ready (the classic sand meanwhile) |
| *On: every grain simulated on the graphics chip* | Real sand runs |
| *Off: the classic sand* | switched off: the classic sand, nothing of Real sand loaded |
| *Needs WebGPU (iOS 26 or later). Tap to try again* | not in this browser: the switch shows off; a tap tries again, and so does a launch seven days on |
| *Could not start this time: (why). It will try again next launch* | the classic sand until then |
| *Did not start 3 times: (why). Turn it off and on to try again* | it stops trying |
| *Real sand is updating; it will be back at the next launch* | old and new files of an update met; the next launch has the new ones |
| *Real sand paused: the graphics chip was reset* | the classic sand took over at once, at the same time and with the same sand above; Real sand comes back by itself |
| *Real sand paused: the graphics chip stopped answering* | the same, when Real sand sent nothing back for 4 s; it starts again 2 s later |
| *Real sand paused: the graphics chip stopped answering three times; it will try again next launch* | the classic sand until the app is opened again |
| *Paused after the graphics chip was reset twice; it will try again next launch* | the classic sand until the app is opened again |
| *Real sand could not restart after the graphics chip was reset; it will try again next launch* | the same, when the chip could not be opened again |
| *Real sand could not restart; it will try again next launch* | the same, when no graphics adapter was found right after the chip was reset or stopped answering: nothing is remembered or counted, and the next launch tries again |

Around a lock or a switch to another app, a reset graphics chip is
rebuilt without a word.

To report how it does: with the switch on, Tune's Sand tab has **Copy a report** under
it. Tap it and paste the report into your message, with what you did (which
glass, the phone still or in the hand, Real motion on or off) and what you
saw. It holds how long the start took, the stream's first fall from the neck
to the floor, the power mode and rate, each heat guard trip and the engine's
log, from the app as you use it, home-screen web app included. Its `ends`
part says, for each glass that ended, when the last grain crossed the neck
against 00:00.0 (in ms, from the graphics chip's own record), and counts any
run that let sand through ahead of the clock, any crossing while the neck
was shut and any failed check of the count (all three should be 0). To watch the
figures live, open the app in Safari with `?debug=sand` after its address
(`https://elkhanany.github.io/hourglass/?debug=sand`): small white figures
at the bottom left show the power mode, the rate, how fast the sand runs
against real time, the sand above the neck, the first fall and the start,
with the same report under **copy the report**. (A web app on the home
screen keeps its own storage and always opens at its own address, so
`?debug=sand` cannot reach it; those figures are Safari's.) For the battery:
run the same 30-minute glass with Real sand on and then off, at a fixed
brightness (Auto-Brightness off), not charging, and note the battery before
and after each.

## Browser notes

- iOS Safari only starts audio from a user gesture. The app creates and resumes
  the audio context on the first touch, and resumes it again after an
  interruption (call, lock screen, tab switch). One touch is enough, also
  one that starts nothing (the glass taken and let go); the sound begins as
  that touch ends, so a glass dragged about in the very first touch of a
  launch is silent until the finger lifts.
- Real motion needs motion permission on iOS 13+; the switch asks for it, once
  for both sensors. An iPhone keeps that permission only until the app is
  closed, and a web app cannot change that, so with Real motion left on it
  has to be given again at each launch. The app does not spring the question
  on the first tap that comes along: a small tilt icon waits in the top bar
  (the first three times a note points to it) and asks when it is tapped.
  Left alone, the glass simply stands still that time and Real motion stays
  on for the next. Where the site is still allowed (the same session, a
  reload) the readings arrive unasked and the icon never shows.
- Keep screen awake needs the Screen Wake Lock API: Safari 16.4+ on iOS, and
  iOS 18.4+ when installed to the home screen.
- A web app cannot hide the iPhone status bar or silence notifications. On
  Android the installed app runs full screen; in desktop and Android browsers
  the moon icon enters full screen.
- Haptics use `navigator.vibrate` on Android. An iPhone has no vibration
  API. Since iOS 18 a switch control taps the Taptic Engine when it flips,
  and since iOS 26.5 only when a finger really lands on it or on its label.
  So on an iPhone every control, and the glass itself, carries a transparent
  label over a hidden switch, and a tap that the app wants felt lets that
  switch flip: one light tick a tap, on buttons, pills, cards, switches, and
  a tap or a knock on the glass. What happens without a tap cannot tick on
  an iPhone: the end of a glass, a landing, a crack from shaking, a catch or
  a half turn while the finger drags (on iOS 18 to 26.4 those still tick as
  the finger lifts). *System Haptics* must be on in the iPhone's Sounds &
  Haptics settings. This is tested in a browser shaped like an iPhone
  (`app-haptics`), which has no Taptic Engine: whether the phone ticks can
  only be felt on a phone.
- The completion sound can only play in a hidden tab if the browser keeps the
  page's audio running; on a locked phone it generally will not. The bowl's
  repeats (*Rings*) are rung only while the app is on screen.
- *Sound in silent mode* uses the page's audio session
  (`navigator.audioSession`, Safari 16.4+): on, it is "playback", which
  sounds with the ringer off; off, it is "ambient", which follows the ringer
  switch and lets other apps' music play on. Other browsers have no audio
  session and decide by themselves; there the switch is kept but changes
  nothing.
